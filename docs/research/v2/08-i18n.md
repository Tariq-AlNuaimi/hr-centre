### next-intl@4 — the i18n library

**One line:** next-intl is a Next.js-native i18n library (ICU MessageFormat, RSC-first, middleware routing). Our current choice — `next-intl@4.13.1`, wired config-driven in `i18n/`.

**4-lens top-10 (React/Next i18n libraries)**

| # | Candidate | Lens | Stars (2026-07) | License | Maturity / latest | One-line why-it-ranks |
|---|-----------|------|-----------------|---------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| 1 | **next-intl** | HS·OSS·BT | 4.3k [1] | MIT | 2020 · v4.13.2 (Jul 10 2026) | Next-native RSC + App-Router routing + ICU, ~2 KB runtime; the App-Router default |
| 2 | **react-intl (FormatJS)** | BT·HS·ENT | 13.8k [4] | BSD-3 | 2015 · active | Reference ICU impl, huge enterprise footprint; ~20 KB, RSC needs `@formatjs/intl` shim |
| 3 | **react-i18next / i18next** | BT·HS·ENT | 10.0k / 8.6k [5][6] | MIT | 2015 · active | Largest ecosystem, plugins/backends/detectors; not RSC-native, `use client` for hooks |
| 4 | **LinguiJS** | OSS·BT | 5.8k [3] | MIT | 2017 · v5 active | Compile-time macros, ~2–5 KB tree-shaken bundle, ICU, App-Router support; extraction step |
| 5 | **Paraglide JS (inlang)** | OSS·HS | ~2k (inlang mono) [2] | MIT | 2023 · v2.21 (2026) | Compiler emits type-safe message *functions*, ~70% smaller bundles, tree-shakeable, RSC-safe |
| 6 | **next-i18next** | BT | ~6.9k [7] | MIT | 2019 · maintenance | i18next-for-Next wrapper — **Pages-Router era**; App-Router users pushed to raw react-i18next |
| 7 | **next-international** | OSS | ~1.8k [8] | MIT | 2022 · active | Fully type-safe, tiny, App-Router-first; no ICU (custom interpolation), smaller community |
| 8 | **typesafe-i18n** | OSS | 1.2k [9] | MIT | 2021 · slowing | 100% type-safe, zero-dep, framework-agnostic; maintenance has cooled, no RSC story |
| 9 | **@formatjs/intl** (headless) | ENT | (part of 13.8k) | BSD-3 | — | Framework-free ICU formatter — the RSC-safe core to pair with any router layer |
| 10 | **Lingui + next-intl hybrids** | — | — | — | — | Niche; not worth the double toolchain |

**Head-to-head.** For **Next 16 App Router + RSC**, the field narrows to four real options: **next-intl**, **Paraglide**, **Lingui**, and **next-international**. next-intl is the only one purpose-built for Next's App Router — it owns locale routing, middleware detection, `setRequestLocale`/static rendering, and ICU, with a ~2 KB runtime and zero `createInstance` boilerplate; its trade-off is runtime JSON message loading (no build-time tree-shaking, so the whole catalogue per-locale ships) and message keys that are only *opt-in* typed (via `global.d.ts` augmentation). **Paraglide** is the modern challenger: its compiler turns messages into individually-imported typed functions, so bundles stay flat as catalogues grow (100 msgs ≈ 47 KB regardless of 200 or 1000 total) and every key is type-checked by construction — but it's a heavier build integration, its ICU/plural story is thinner, and its Next adapter (`@inlang/paraglide-next`) trails next-intl's Next-team-adjacent polish. **Lingui** gives compile-time extraction + tiny bundles + real ICU, but the macro/extraction workflow is more ceremony than a config-driven template wants. **react-intl** and **react-i18next** are the battle-tested enterprise incumbents with the biggest ecosystems, but both are Pages-Router-shaped: neither is RSC-native, both need `use client` for their hooks, and react-intl's ~20 KB runtime is the heaviest here. **next-i18next is a current-events trap** — it's effectively Pages-Router legacy; its own guidance now sends App-Router users to raw react-i18next, so adopting it for a Next 16 repo is a step backward. **typesafe-i18n** has cooled (no credible RSC path).

**Recommendation: KEEP (Fit 5/5).** next-intl is the correct, best-in-class choice for this exact stack — RSC-native, ICU, config-driven locale routing already wired cleanly in `i18n/`. No enterprise gap justifies a migration: react-intl/react-i18next would *regress* RSC support, and Paraglide's only real win (bundle tree-shaking) doesn't bite until catalogues are large and per-tenant deploys already split traffic. **KEEP+HARDEN the one soft spot — type-safety:** turn on next-intl's typed-messages augmentation so missing/renamed keys fail typecheck (add a `messages` type declaration referencing `en.json`), which closes the gap that makes Paraglide/next-international attractive. If a future tenant ships a 1000+-key catalogue and bundle weight shows up in Speed-Insights, revisit **Paraglide as an ADD-AS-OPTION** (compiler tree-shaking), not a default swap. **ADR impact:** none required to keep; a one-line ADR note to record "typed-messages on" hardening.

### RTL handling posture

**One line:** How the repo renders right-to-left (Arabic/Hebrew/Persian/Urdu). Current: `directionForLocale(locale)` sets `<html dir>` in `app/[locale]/layout.tsx`, propagated to Radix via `DirectionProvider` in `ThemeProvider`.

**Best-practice checklist (2026) vs. our posture**

| Practice | Best-practice (2026) [10][11][12] | Our repo | Verdict |
|----------|-----------------------------------|----------|---------|
| Direction source | `dir` on the **`<html>` root only**, never per-element | `dir={direction}` on `<html>` in `[locale]/layout.tsx` | ✅ correct |
| Derivation | direction from locale, not hardcoded | `RTL_LOCALES` set → `directionForLocale` (auto `ar/he/fa/ur`) | ✅ correct, config-driven |
| Component libs | pass direction to headless primitives | Radix `DirectionProvider dir={direction}` in `ThemeProvider` | ✅ correct |
| CSS strategy | **CSS logical properties** (`margin-inline-*`, `ps-`/`pe-`/`ms-`/`me-`) — fixes ~80% of RTL | Tailwind v4 (logical utilities available) — **enforce, don't audit-later** | ⚠️ needs a lint gate |
| Tailwind variants | `rtl:`/`ltr:` variants activate off ancestor `dir` | available in TW v4 | ✅ available |
| Icon flipping | flip *directional* icons (arrows/chevrons); **never blanket `scaleX(-1)`** (breaks logos, checkmarks, media) | lucide-react; needs a documented convention | ⚠️ document convention |
| Arabic type | body 18–20 px (vs 15–16 Latin), line-height 1.6–1.8 | not codified | ⚠️ add to design tokens/recipe |

**Head-to-head (approaches).** The winning posture — set `dir` at the root + drive everything off CSS logical properties — is exactly what modern browsers/Tailwind v4 make cheap, and the repo already nails the *structural* half (root `dir`, config-derived direction, Radix `DirectionProvider`). The remaining risk is **physical-property drift**: any `ml-*/pl-*/left-*` written by a fork silently breaks RTL, and nothing currently *prevents* it. The `no-raw-hex-color` local ESLint rule is the exact precedent for closing this — a `no-physical-inset-utility` rule (flag `ml-/mr-/pl-/pr-/left-/right-` in favor of `ms-/me-/ps-/pe-/start-/end-`) would make RTL-correctness a gate, not a QA finding, consistent with the Five-Pillars "gates not vibes" doctrine.

**Recommendation: KEEP+HARDEN (Fit 5/5).** Structure is already best-practice. Harden with: (1) a local ESLint rule banning physical inset/margin/padding utilities in favor of logical ones (mirrors `no-raw-hex-color`); (2) a documented icon-flip convention (directional-only, wrap in an `rtl:-scale-x-100` utility class applied deliberately — never globally); (3) Arabic typography defaults (min body size + line-height) in the design tokens / `design-recipes.md`. **ADR impact:** small ADR for the new ESLint rule (it joins the 10-rule security/quality set).

### Translation management platform (NET-NEW — none yet)

**One line:** A TMS/localization workflow for translators (in-context editing, MT, review, sync to `messages/*.json`). NET-NEW — the repo has raw JSON catalogues and no management layer.

**4-lens top-10 (self-hostable TMS + enterprise cloud)**

| # | Candidate | Lens | Stars (2026-07) | License | Model | One-line why-it-ranks |
|---|-----------|------|-----------------|---------|-------|-----------------------|
| 1 | **Weblate** | BT·HS·OSS | ~6.0k [13][14] | GPL-3.0 | Self-host / cloud | Most mature OSS TMS (2012), git-first, battle-tested, huge format support incl. ICU/JSON |
| 2 | **Tolgee** | OSS·HS | ~4.0k [13][14] | Apache-2.0 (+Tolgee EL for some enterprise bits) | Self-host / cloud | Dev-first, **in-context editing**, SDK for JS/React, MT + AI, clean self-host; newest & fastest-moving |
| 3 | **Crowdin** | ENT·BT | (proprietary) | Commercial | Cloud (Enterprise self-host) | 700+ integrations (GitHub/GitLab/Figma/Slack), largest workflow ecosystem; not OSS |
| 4 | **Lokalise** | ENT | (proprietary) | Commercial | Cloud | Enterprise TMS, SAML/SSO + SCIM on higher tiers, seat-priced; polished but locked-in |
| 5 | **Localazy** | ENT | (proprietary) | Commercial | Cloud | Automation + MT marketplace, CLI/SDKs; smaller, developer-friendly cloud |
| 6 | **Pontoon** (Mozilla) | OSS·BT | ~3k | BSD-3 | Self-host | Mozilla's own TMS, proven at scale; heavier ops, Django |
| 7 | **Phrase (Strings)** | ENT·BT | (proprietary) | Commercial | Cloud | Enterprise incumbent, strong compliance/SLA; expensive, not self-host |
| 8 | **Transifex** | ENT·BT | (proprietary) | Commercial | Cloud | Long-standing enterprise TMS, good API; proprietary |
| 9 | **Traduora** | OSS | ~3k | AGPL-3.0 | Self-host | Simple self-host TMS; slower maintenance, AGPL caution |
| 10 | **Accent / simple git-JSON** | OSS | small | MIT | Self-host / none | Lightweight; for tiny catalogues raw JSON + PR review may beat a TMS |

**Head-to-head.** The enterprise pivot (self-hosted, data-sovereign, deploy-per-tenant, Docker-first) filters this hard: the **cloud incumbents (Crowdin/Lokalise/Localazy/Phrase/Transifex) are proprietary and mostly not self-hostable**, so they fail the sovereignty test even where their workflows (Crowdin's 700+ integrations, Lokalise's SSO/SCIM) are best-in-class. That leaves two credible self-host picks: **Weblate** and **Tolgee**. **Weblate** is the safer, more battle-tested choice — GPL-3.0, git-native (it commits translations back to the repo, matching our "messages live in the repo" model), broadest format support, and a decade of production use — at the cost of a heavier Django/Postgres/Redis ops footprint and a more translator-than-developer UX. **Tolgee** is the better *developer* fit — Apache-2.0, an in-context editor that overlays the running app, a first-class JS/React SDK and CLI to sync `messages/*.json`, and native ICU/AI-MT — but its most enterprise-shaped features sit under the "Tolgee EL" source-available license (verify SSO/air-gap terms before committing), and it's younger. For a repo whose translations are already git-committed JSON and whose audience is developer-operators, **Tolgee's git+in-context+SDK loop is the closest match**; Weblate wins if strict OSS licensing (no source-available clauses) or maximal maturity is the hard constraint.

**Recommendation: ADD-AS-OPTION → Tolgee (self-hosted), Weblate as the pure-GPL alternative (Fit 4/5).** Don't hard-wire a TMS into the template — most single-locale forks never need one. Ship it as a **`/scale-up` recipe** ("localization workflow"): default English-only forks stay on raw JSON + PR review; a fork that turns on `ar` (or ≥2 real translation locales) adopts **Tolgee self-hosted** via docker-compose, synced to `messages/*.json` through its CLI in CI. Recommend **Weblate** instead when the tenant mandates a strict copyleft/no-source-available license or wants the most-proven option. Avoid the proprietary cloud TMSs unless a specific tenant already runs one. **ADR impact:** new ADR "localization workflow (Tolgee self-host, Weblate alt)"; new `/scale-up` recipe row; net-new deps are **container-side only** (no npm runtime dep — CLI is dev/CI), so no bundle or Ask-before-dep impact.
